## Definition

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics produces statistics in a variety of formats. Below are key definitions for central tendency and distribution.

Average, mean, or arithmetic mean is the sum of a set of numbers divided by the number of members in the set.

Median or 50th percentile is the midpoint of a set of values. This is the point where half of the values are greater and half are less than the value.

Percentile refers to the proportion, or percent, of a distribution that is less than the nth percentile. For example, if the wages at the 20th percentile are $10.00, then 20 percent of the wages are less than $10.00.

Quartile divides a distribution into four equal segments. The lowest quartile spans from the lowest value to the 25th percentile; the second from the 25th percentile to the 50th percentile; the third from the 50th percentile to the 75th percentile; and the highest from the 75th percentile to the highest value.

Quintile divides a distribution into five equal segments. Decile divides a distribution into 10 equal segments.

Sometimes a mean does not convey much information, particularly if there is a wide distribution of values. Learning about the distribution of wages can give more information about what an entry-level wage may look like compared with a wage with more experience.
